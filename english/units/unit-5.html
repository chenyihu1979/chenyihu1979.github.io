Page 68 · When the earth stands still · Paragraph 1
1 Have you ever thought about this question: what would happen to the natural world if people were gone? Let's imagine it. One day, all human activities stop. Cars disappear from the roads, boats from the rivers, and planes from the sky. Streets, beaches and parks that are usually full of people remain empty. What happens in such a world?
Pages 68–69 · When the earth stands still · Paragraph 2
2 It is no surprise that animals soon increase in number. In cities, traffic noise often means that animals cannot easily hear each other. Without humans, the noise drops greatly as traffic stops. This allows natural sounds to fill the air. It is much easier for animals to find each other and form families. Also, tourists disappear from beaches and seas. Sea animals are not troubled by people around them.
Page 69 · When the earth stands still · Paragraph 3
3 But not only animals enjoy the change. Plants benefit, too. Trees grow freely without the danger of being cut down. Forests take back the space that humans now live in. The earth is soon a green world again.
Page 69 · When the earth stands still · Paragraph 4
4 The natural wonders which have been hidden by pollution for years appear again. Cleaner air and water allow them to show all their beauty once again. But there aren't any people to admire such an amazing world. What a sad thing to think about!
Page 69 · When the earth stands still · Paragraph 5
5 Throughout history, humans have left so many marks on the earth. The rapid development of human society has made us care less for nature. But the world isn't a game. We can't cancel it and start over. We need to think more about how to get along with nature. Perhaps we should remember the wise words which Montaigne once wrote — let us permit nature to have her way.

Page 76 · No longer "in danger" · Paragraph 1
1 The panda is a Chinese national treasure, and has become popular around the world. Some foreigners even believe that pandas are kept as pets in China! In fact, back in the 1970s, there were only around 1,000 of these lovely animals in the wild. In 1961, the World Wildlife Fund (WWF) was looking for an animal for its logo. The panda was chosen because it is "an animal that is beautiful, endangered, and loved by many people in the world".
Page 76 · No longer "in danger" · Paragraph 2
2 But in the year 2016, there came some fantastic news — the panda was moved from "endangered" to "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. When people around the world celebrated, they also wondered, "How did this happen?"
Pages 76–77 · No longer "in danger" · Paragraph 3
3 China's government has made great efforts over the years. Among the first national parks in China, there was one for panda protection. The Giant Panda National Park is three times as large as Yellowstone National Park in the United States. Since the 1960s, China has created more than 60 protected areas for pandas. Research centres have also been set up and hundreds of pandas have been born there.
Page 77 · No longer "in danger" · Paragraph 4
4 Local people have also helped. With a better understanding of panda protection, many farmers returned part of their farmland to the forests. They planted trees and bamboo, so pandas could have more space and food.
Page 77 · No longer "in danger" · Paragraph 5
5 The panda is not the only animal that has benefited from these actions. The forests that they live in are also home to many other animals and plants. As the forests become larger, the panda's neighbours also enjoy a better life. Protecting pandas brings hope to the whole environment.
